# Degree Planning and Graduation Requirements

Scope: What an undergraduate must complete to earn the B.A., how general education and the Area of Concentration work, the thesis and baccalaureate examination milestones, transfer credit limits, and the parallel graduate degree structure. Deadlines are cross-referenced to `resources/shared/academic-calendar.md`; the underlying contract/evaluation system is explained in `academic-model.md`.

Verified through: 2026-08-31

Applies to: undergraduate students under catalog edition 2025-2026, plus published graduate degree structures. Requirements follow the catalog at admission or at graduation.

Volatility: `annual`.

---

## Catalog year applicability — read this first

> "Students must meet requirements as stated in the Undergraduate Catalog at the time of their admission or in the Catalog for the year in which they graduate."

Consequences for answering:

- A requirements question is unanswerable without an entry year or an intended graduation year. Ask for one when it changes the answer.
- General education is explicitly tied to entry year: the degree requirements list "the General Education requirements **as determined by the students' year of entry**."
- At the verification date the published catalog edition was **2025-2026** while the current academic year was **2026-2027**. A student entering in Fall 2026 may be governed by an edition not yet published publicly. Say so rather than guessing.

## B.A. degree requirements

The undergraduate catalog lists these requirements for the Bachelor of Arts:

1. **Seven satisfactory semester contracts**
2. **Three satisfactory Independent Study Projects (ISPs)**
3. **31 academic units** — "a unit being equivalent to a full-semester 4 credit course or an ISP"
4. **General Education requirements** as determined by the student's year of entry
5. **Demonstrated competency in Civic Literacy**
6. **A satisfactory Senior Project or Thesis**, "accepted in final form by Library staff"
7. **A satisfactory Baccalaureate Examination**, "usually in the form of an oral defense of the senior project"

The catalog states that the A.A. degree requires "at least 7.5 academic units completed in residence." A comparable residency figure for the B.A. is **not stated** on the degree requirements page. Do not supply one.

**Graduation application.** The academic calendar publishes a graduation application deadline — Fri, Oct 2, 2026 for May 2027 graduates. The degree requirements page does not describe the application procedure itself; route to the Office of the Registrar.

## General education

The general education page states the requirement as **"36 credit hours of approved general education coursework"**, composed of:

**State core (5 areas):**

| Area | As published |
|---|---|
| Humanities | 1 course (4 credits) — HUM 1020 (Odyssey) |
| Natural Sciences | 1 course — biology, chemistry, physics, environmental studies, or astronomy options |
| Civic Literacy | "one course plus a passing grade on the Florida Civic Literacy Exam" |
| Mathematics | 1 course — algebra, statistics, or calculus options |
| Communication | 1 course — ENC 1101 English Composition or approved alternatives |

**New College components:**

| Component | As published |
|---|---|
| Introduction to Techne | 1 course (ISC 2076), described as a 2-credit course |
| Enduring Human Questions (EHQ) seminars | "four offerings ... (16 credits)" |

**Grading restriction:** "The Pass/Fail No Credit grading option is not available for general education courses."

> **Unresolved arithmetic.** The page states 36 credit hours, but the itemized components as published (five state-core courses at 4 credits, a 2-credit Techne course, and 16 credits of EHQ seminars) appear to total 38. This resource does not reconcile the difference and does not assume which figure is correct. A student planning against an exact credit total should confirm with the Office of the Registrar or their sponsor.

**Civic Literacy** appears twice — as a general education area and as a separate degree requirement. It requires both a course and a passing score on the Florida Civic Literacy Exam.

## Area of Concentration (AOC)

The AOC is the New College equivalent of a major. The catalog describes seven forms:

**Single:**

1. **Liberal Arts** — endorsement from two faculty in different divisions; study across all three divisions.
2. **Divisional** — within Humanities, Natural Sciences, or Social Sciences; two faculty endorsements (three for Social Sciences).
3. **Programmatic** — a discipline or interdisciplinary program; two faculty endorsements.
4. **Special Program** — custom interdisciplinary title; three faculty endorsements plus descriptive documentation.

**Combined:**

5. **Joint Concentration** — two disciplines, "at least 7 units" in each; three faculty endorsements.
6. **Double AOC** — two full concentrations; separate or unified thesis.
7. **AOC with Secondary Field (minor).**

**Unit floor:** "a minimum requirement of 10 academic units" for an AOC; minors are typically 5 units.

### AOC milestones

| Milestone | Catalog rule | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Provisional AOC | "By the middle of the fourth attempted academic contract" | PAOC Plan |
| Thesis Prospectus / AOC | "By 5:00 p.m. on the first Friday of Module II during the sixth contract" | Thesis Prospectus/AOC Form |

The **Thesis Prospectus/AOC Form** requires at least three faculty signatures: two certifying the concentration (one of whom serves as thesis sponsor), plus one additional faculty member from any division who joins the baccalaureate committee.

Consequence worth stating to students: "A student must submit this form to the Office of the Registrar in order to enroll for the following semester." A missing AOC form blocks registration.

Extensions exist for "First-time-in-college students with Dual Enrollment contract transfer credit," who should contact the Office of the Registrar.

> **Conflict: milestone timing.** The catalog anchors these milestones to contract number (fourth contract, sixth contract). The academic calendar anchors them to semester number and gives fixed dates — "PAOC Submission Deadline (5th Semester)" and "Thesis Prospectus Submission Deadline (6th Semester)" on Mon, Nov 2, 2026 and Mon, Apr 19, 2027. Two problems follow: the PAOC is tied to the *fourth* contract in the catalog and the *fifth* semester on the calendar, and the catalog's "first Friday of Module II" for Fall 2026 would be Fri, Oct 16, 2026 rather than the calendar's Nov 2, 2026. This is unresolved in public sources. Present both, and tell the student the Office of the Registrar controls the operative date.

## Minors and certificates

The catalog lists roughly forty minors — among them Applied Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, Computer Science, Data Science, Economics, Finance, History, Literature, Marine Biology, Philosophy, Physics, Political Science, Psychology, Statistics, and Theatre, Dance and Performance — and four undergraduate certificates: Bloomberg Market Concepts, Chartered Financial Analyst, Geographic Information Systems, and Innovative Digital Media.

**Coverage gap:** the minors and certificates page does not publish unit requirements or declaration deadlines for each program. The AOC page's figure of "typically 5 units" for a minor is the only unit guidance found. Route students to the individual program page, their sponsor, and the Office of the Registrar.

## Senior project / thesis

Every undergraduate completes a senior project or thesis regardless of AOC, and it must be "accepted in final form by Library staff."

- **Submission is electronic:** a PDF uploaded through the Thesis Submission Form on the library website. Physical copies are optional and need not be signed.
- **Non-written projects** (artwork, performance) require a written description plus supporting materials — slides, video, or recordings.
- **Sponsor approval** is signified by the faculty sponsor's signature on the abstract.
- **Deadline:** the final copy must reach the library "by the Monday of graduation week by no later than 5:00 p.m."
- **Consequence of missing it,** quoted: "the student will not be able to participate in the College's graduation ceremony, his or her diploma will be withheld, and a hold will be placed on his or her transcript until the library copy has been accepted."
- Questions: thesis@ncf.edu.

Calendar dates for 2026-2027: thesis due to library Mon, Aug 3, 2026 (August graduates); Fri, Jan 22, 2027 (January graduates); thesis submissions and thesis report deadline Mon, May 17, 2027 (May graduates).

## Baccalaureate examination

The baccalaureate examination is "an oral defense of the student's performance in three areas: the senior thesis, the Area(s) of Concentration, and the student's undergraduate education in general." Three faculty members conduct it and must approve the quality of the student's achievement before graduation.

- **Timing:** "usually is held two or three weeks prior to the semester's end, but examinations can be scheduled earlier." The academic calendar reserves Baccalaureate/Reading Days — Mon–Wed, Apr 26–28, 2027 for Spring 2027.
- **Prerequisite:** the thesis must be complete and the AOC substantially finished.
- **Report deadlines (2026-2027):** Baccalaureate Exam Report due Fri, Jul 31, 2026; Wed, Jan 20, 2027 for January graduates; Baccalaureate Examination Reports (BER) due Fri, May 14, 2027.

**Coverage gap:** the public baccalaureate examination page does not enumerate possible outcomes (for example, pass with distinction, pass, fail, or a re-examination path). Do not invent an outcome scale.

## Transfer credit

- **Maximum:** 60 semester credit hours, "equivalent to 15 units."
- Reached via a Florida College System or State University System AA degree, a bachelor's or advanced degree from an accredited institution, or 60 transferable credits from any accredited source.
- The catalog translates the maximum as **"3 semester contracts, 1 Independent Study Project, and 2 additional units (8 credit hours)."**
- **Credit by exam** is awarded per the Articulation Coordinating Committee Credit-by-Exam Guidelines (AP, IB, AICE, CLEP).
- **Partial contract or ISP credit is not awarded.**
- Exemptions from contracts or requirements "do not guarantee exemptions from Area of Concentration requirements or other degree requirements."
- Official transcripts are required; unofficial transcripts and grade reports are not accepted.
- The catalog warns that even with maximum credit a student may need "more than four semesters" to finish.
- Denials may be appealed through the Registrar, then the Provost.

Transfer students are also exempt from the recommendation that the first ISP be taken on campus — see `isp-and-experiential-learning.md`.

## Graduate degree structure

Two graduate programs are published:

- **M.S. in Applied Data Science** — "36 credit hours of graduate work," comprising "11 courses at 3 credit hours each, one practicum at 3 credit hours, and one non-credit earning course," across a structured two-year curriculum. "All graduate students are required to enroll full-time beginning in the fall semester. There are no part-time or spring enrollment options." A minimum 3.00 cumulative GPA is required by the end of the program.
- **M.S. in Marine Mammal Science.**

Graduate students have no contracts, no ISP requirement, no AOC, and no baccalaureate examination. Do not apply undergraduate milestones to them.

## What this resource does not establish

- Whether a specific student has met any requirement. That is a degree audit, and it belongs to the Office of the Registrar.
- Whether a course satisfies a specific general education area in a given catalog year.
- Whether faculty will endorse a proposed AOC.
- Unit requirements for individual minors and certificates.

# Financial and Status Cautions

Scope: General public warnings about how academic decisions — dropping a course, withdrawing, taking a leave, studying off campus, ceasing to attend — can affect financial aid, veterans benefits, visa status, and graduation. This resource explains published rules and names the office that decides. It never decides an individual case.

Verified through: 2026-08-31

Applies to: undergraduate students under catalog edition 2025-2026; dates are from the 2026-2027 public academic calendar.

Volatility: `annual` for aid program rules; `term` for the dates.

---

## The single most important rule for this topic

**The bot does not determine anyone's aid, benefits, status, or eligibility.** It states the published rule, states that consequences depend on the individual record, and names the office that must confirm: the Office of Financial Aid for aid, the Office of the Registrar for enrollment and VA certification, the Designated School Official for immigration, and Student Accounts for billing.

Do not ask for or accept award amounts, EFC/SAI figures, account balances, visa details, or household financial information. If a student volunteers them, do not use them to compute anything.

---

## Full-time status and the 12-credit line

Full-time enrollment for aid purposes is "continued registration in at least 12 credit hours (3 units) per semester, **not including** the January Independent Study Project (ISP)."

Two consequences students routinely miss:

1. **The ISP does not count toward the 12 credits.** A student who plans to satisfy full-time status with the ISP is mistaken.
2. **Dropping one 4-credit course from a 12-credit contract drops the student below full time**, because 12 credits is the floor, not a cushion. The catalog warns that dropping below full-time enrollment may result in "cancellation and/or adjustment of all funding sources."

Institutional scholarships and need-based grants require both full-time enrollment **and** active attendance.

## Withdrawal, dropping, and repayment

**Federal Title IV aid.** A student who withdraws before the 60% point of the semester is subject to a recalculation and must return unearned assistance.

**Florida Bright Futures.** Students who withdraw, drop, or stop attending must repay Bright Futures funds for that term — the catalog states the repayment is "in full" for the term.

**Institutional aid** may also require partial or full repayment.

**General warning, quoted:** "If you withdraw, you may be responsible for repaying all or part of your financial aid and/or scholarships."

**Ceasing to attend is not neutral.** Stopping attendance without officially withdrawing triggers the same financial aid consequences as a formal withdrawal, and the College sets the last date of attendance from the earliest documented date. Always tell a student who describes "just not going anymore" to speak with Financial Aid and the Registrar before, not after.

**Loan grace periods** may be "backdated to the time you stop attending classes," and exit counseling is required.

**Refund dates published for 2026-2027:** last day for a 25% tuition/fees refund on a full withdrawal is Fri, Sep 11, 2026 (Fall 2026) and Wed, Feb 24, 2027 (Spring 2027). The catalog's refund policy separately describes a 100% refund for withdrawal "prior to, or during, the second week of classes" and does not publish a full graduated schedule. These two sources do not combine into a complete refund table — say so and route to Student Accounts and the Registrar.

**Non-refundable items:** enrollment and housing deposits, late registration fees, and late payment fees. First-time students forfeit the $200 admission deposit even when they qualify for a 100% refund.

**ISP tuition** is refundable only if the student withdraws "by the end of the add/drop period" — Wed, Jan 6, 2027 for the January 2027 Interterm.

## Aid application dates

- **FAFSA priority deadline: February 1**, for both incoming and continuing students.
- A student must have an offer of admission before aid can be offered.

**Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) for aid** is a separate policy from academic standing. The undergraduate catalog references an external SAP policy document rather than restating the rules; the graduate catalog publishes a SAP policy page. Do not conflate SAP with the academic standing rules in `academic-model.md` — a student can be in good academic standing and still fail SAP, or the reverse. Route SAP questions to the Office of Financial Aid.

## Off-campus study and aid

A student studying off campus must arrange for the host institution's official transcript to reach `registrar@ncf.edu` immediately after completion. If the transcript is not received by the published deadline, the semester's aid may be removed from the student's account. The published transcript deadline for summer coursework is the last day of fall classes — Fri, Dec 4, 2026.

## Veterans and military-affiliated students

- The **School Certifying Official (SCO)** in the Office of the Registrar handles VA certification and "serves as the liaison between the College and the VA," transmitting certifications and enrollment verification.
- To start benefits a student provides "a VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE)," "a written request to be certified," and any additional information needed for enrollment certification.
- The **Office of Financial Aid** answers questions about how VA benefits, including the Yellow Ribbon Program, affect aid and billing.
- Registrar phone for veterans and military-affiliated services and for military training credit: (941) 487-4230.
- Under the Veterans Benefits and Transition Act (38 U.S.C. § 3679), the College may not penalize an eligible student for delayed VA disbursement, until payment arrives or "90 days after the date the College certified tuition and fees."
- Discharged veterans may need the DD 214 for state-specific benefits.

**Caution to state:** VA benefits are certified against enrolled credit hours, so any schedule change can change certification. The public page does not spell out the penalty for a specific change. Tell the student to contact the SCO **before** dropping, not after.

## International students and F-1 status

- Students requiring an F-1 visa "must complete all requirements and submit their enrollment deposit by May 1 to allow adequate time for visa processing." Separate deadlines apply to students transferring an active SEVIS record from another U.S. institution.
- English proficiency requirements "vary by entry level."
- Transferring students "should work closely with their current and new Designated School Officials (DSOs)."

**Coverage gap and caution.** The public admission page found at the verification date **does not state a full-time enrollment requirement for F-1 students** or name an international student advisor. Immigration status rules are federal, and a course drop can affect them. Do not state a credit threshold for F-1 status, do not interpret status rules, and do not ask about a student's immigration situation. Direct the student to a Designated School Official before making any enrollment change.

---

## Graduation and status consequences of delay

- A missing Thesis Prospectus/AOC form blocks enrollment for the following semester — "A student must submit this form to the Office of the Registrar in order to enroll for the following semester."
- Missing the library thesis deadline means no participation in the ceremony, a withheld diploma, and a transcript hold until the copy is accepted.
- A contract not submitted by 5:00 p.m. on the second Friday of the semester results in canceled enrollment absent a successful Provost petition.
- Returning from a leave of absence ordinarily places a student on academic probation.

These are published consequences, not predictions. State them as what the source says will happen, and name the office that can confirm the student's actual situation.

## What this resource does not establish

- Any individual aid award, repayment amount, balance, or eligibility determination.
- Whether a specific drop or withdrawal will cost a specific student their aid or benefits.
- Any immigration determination or F-1 credit threshold.
- SAP status.
- Whether an appeal, petition, or waiver will be granted.

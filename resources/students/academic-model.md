# The New College Academic Model

Scope: How academic work is structured, measured, and evaluated at New College of Florida: the semester contract, units and credits, evaluation terminology, academic standing, and the advising relationship. This resource explains the system. Specific dates live in `resources/shared/academic-calendar.md`; graduation requirements live in `degree-planning.md`.

Verified through: 2026-08-31

Applies to: undergraduate students under catalog edition 2025-2026, with a separate section for graduate students. Requirements are catalog-year dependent.

Volatility: `annual`.

---

## Why this model is unusual

New College does not run on a conventional GPA-and-course-count model for undergraduates. Two features drive almost every student question:

1. **The semester contract.** Enrollment is organized around a negotiated contract with a faculty sponsor, not just a course schedule.
2. **Narrative evaluation.** Instructors write narrative evaluations and assign satisfactory/unsatisfactory designations; letter grades exist alongside them.

A student who is new, transferring in, or coming from another institution frequently does not know these terms. Translate them.

---

## Units and credits

- **One unit = one full-semester course = 4 credits.** A modular (half-semester) course is **half a unit = 2 credits**.
- Undergraduate students "are expected to enroll in, and remain enrolled in, at least three units (12 credits) each semester."
- **Full-time status for financial aid** is defined as "continued registration in at least 12 credit hours (3 units) per semester, **not including** the January Independent Study Project (ISP)." The ISP does not count toward that 12-credit full-time figure.
- Modules: "Module I courses meet for the first seven weeks of a semester. Module II courses meet for the final seven weeks of a semester."

**Do not compute a student's unit total for them.** Unit counts on a real record depend on transfer evaluation, drops, and incompletes. Point to the Office of the Registrar.

## The academic contract

An academic contract "represents one full-time semester of enrollment" and must state educational activities, short- and long-term goals, descriptions of academic work, and the criteria by which it will be certified. Per the catalog it lists "courses, tutorials, internships and other planned academic undertakings, totaling at least three (3) units."

**The contract sponsor.** "The New College faculty member with whom a student develops the contract, and who signs it, is the contract sponsor." The sponsor mentors the student through the semester and **need not teach any of the student's courses**. The sponsor certifies the contract at the end of the term against the criteria written into it.

**Contract certification outcomes:** *Satisfactory*, *Incomplete*, or *Unsatisfactory*.

**Contract renegotiation.** A contract may be revised after submission through renegotiation. The catalog states the deadline for adding or dropping courses is "Friday of the second week of the semester," and there is a separate, later renegotiation deadline published on the academic calendar (Fri, Nov 13, 2026 for Fall 2026; Fri, Apr 23, 2027 for Spring 2027).

**Deadline structure, as published for Fall 2026:**

| Step | Catalog rule | Fall 2026 date |
|---|---|---|
| Contract due | "the first Friday of the semester" | Fri, Aug 21, 2026 |
| Late submission accepted (fee) | "until close of business on the second Friday of the semester" | Fri, Aug 28, 2026 |
| Enrollment canceled if not submitted | "5:00 p.m. on the second Friday of the semester ... unless the student successfully petitions the Provost" | Fri, Aug 28, 2026, 5:00 p.m. |

That last consequence is severe and frequently surprises students. State it plainly and route the student to the Office of the Registrar rather than assuming a petition will succeed.

## Evaluation terminology

Each instructor submits a **narrative evaluation** — the catalog describes these as providing "rich, contextualized, individualized feedback" with "detailed information on the assignments and requirements of every course and on all aspects of course performance."

Unless the student has elected Pass/Fail, the instructor also assigns one designation:

| Designation | Short form | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Strong Satisfactory | Sat+ | Passing |
| Satisfactory | Sat | Passing |
| Marginal Satisfactory | Sat− | Passing |
| Unsatisfactory | Unsat | Failing |
| Incomplete | I | Not yet resolved |

The catalog states: "Passing grades are Strong Satisfactory (A) through Marginal Satisfactory (C). Failing grades are Unsatisfactory (F) and Fail/No Credit (FNC)."

**Letter grades.** Designations are converted to letter grades A–F, but the catalog states "There is no official policy on the relation between the designation ... and the percentage grade," and requires instructors to explain their method in the syllabus. Do not tell a student what percentage corresponds to a designation.

**Incompletes.** An "I" is "treated as an F for GPA calculations until resolved with a final grade, at which time the GPA will be updated accordingly."

**Pass/Fail.** A student may elect P/FNC for **one elective course per term**. "P is equal to a C (2.0) or better" and is not computed in the GPA. "Courses taken to fulfill the general education requirements cannot be taken P/F." Election requires good standing and approval before the add/drop deadline.

Note the two-layer effect: a student can be passing every course (all Sat−) and still be in trouble at the contract level if the sponsor's certification criteria were not met. Contract certification and course designations are different judgments.

## Academic standing (undergraduate)

**Good standing** requires all three: "contract certification criteria are being met, Independent Study Projects (ISPs) satisfactorily completed, and a minimum 2.0 cumulative GPA is maintained."

**Probation** is triggered by "One unsatisfactory contract or two unsatisfactory ISPs." The Registrar notifies the student. The **Student Academic Standing Committee (SASC)** reviews progress at the end of the semester. Appeals are limited to procedural grounds — error or misunderstanding.

The catalog is explicit that partial progress is not enough: SASC looks for "satisfactory evaluations of academic activities included on the semester contract and a satisfactory contract certification," and "Evaluations of 'incomplete' and marginally satisfactory evaluations do not constitute such evidence."

**Dismissal** follows from any of:

- "Two unsatisfactory contracts, which need not be consecutive"
- "One unsatisfactory contract and two unsatisfactory ISPs"
- "One unsatisfactory contract, for students who were readmitted after having previously been dismissed"

A dismissal hearing involves the student, the faculty sponsor, and SASC; **the Provost's Office makes the final determination**.

**Never tell a student what their standing is or will be.** Explain the published thresholds, then route to the Office of the Registrar and their faculty sponsor.

> Naming note: public sources use both "Student Academic Standing Committee" and "Student Academic Status Committee" (the latter appears on the Leave of Absence page) with the same abbreviation, SASC. This resource does not resolve which is the current official name.

## Graduate students are governed by different rules

The graduate catalog uses a conventional letter-grade and GPA model, not contracts and narrative evaluations:

- Letter scale A (4.00) through F (0.00), with IN, NGR, W, XX not calculated in GPA. Non-credit courses are graded Satisfactory / Unsatisfactory.
- "Graduate students must maintain a minimum 3.00 cumulative grade point average to be eligible to earn a degree."
- Falling below 3.0 or failing a course leads to probation; the **Graduate Student Academic Status Committee** reviews at semester's end and may "remove probation, continue probation, or dismiss the student."
- "Any student failing more than one course at any time during the program is subject to dismissal." Appeals go to the Provost, whose decision is final.
- Courses graded F must be repeated. For a repeat, "only the most recent letter grade of C or higher will be factored into the cumulative GPA calculation," though all attempts remain on the transcript.

**Resolve program level before answering anything about standing, grading, or the meaning of a term.** "Am I on probation?" has two different rule sets behind it.

## Advising

The advising page describes a team model: "Your academic sponsor will serve at the center of your advising team," guiding the student "from your initial mini-classes to your baccalaureate exam," and covering "coursework, internships, areas of concentration and senior project." Students also work with "your assigned career coach and any additional student support services."

**Published rules vs. advisor judgment.** The catalog and calendar establish what is required and when. The sponsor decides what counts as satisfactory for *that student's* contract, and signs forms. When a question turns on judgment — whether a project is enough, whether an AOC plan will be endorsed — say so and send the student to their sponsor.

**Coverage gap.** The public advising page states that detailed advising resources and faculty advisor guidelines "can be found on the NCF Community Pages (MyNCF login required)." That material is not publicly available and is therefore out of scope. Do not reconstruct it.

## Catalog year controls

"Students must meet requirements as stated in the Undergraduate Catalog at the time of their admission or in the Catalog for the year in which they graduate."

This means a student may have a genuine choice between two catalog editions, and the answer to a requirements question depends on which one applies. Always establish entry year (or intended graduation year) before answering a requirements question, and say which edition you used.

At the verification date, the published catalog was the **2025-2026** edition while the current academic year was **2026-2027**. Flag this when a student asks about "the current catalog."

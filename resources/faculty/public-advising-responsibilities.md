# Public Faculty Advising and Sponsor Responsibilities

Scope: what New College of Florida states publicly about what a faculty advisor
(contract sponsor) is expected to do, how often they are expected to meet advisees,
and where the advisor's judgment stops and an office's authority begins. This file
covers responsibilities only. Procedures and systems live in
`resources/faculty/academic-workflows.md` and
`resources/faculty/deadlines-and-systems.md`.

**Verified through: 2026-08-31**

**Audience:** faculty. **Applicability:** undergraduate contract system.
**Freshness:** the operating guidance below comes from a Provost's Office page
labeled for 2025-2026 and from a Faculty Handbook dated August 2023. Where the two
differ, this file says so rather than blending them.

---

## The sponsor's place in the advising structure

New College describes advising as a team around the student rather than a single
person. The student's academic sponsor sits at the center of that team; a career
coach and other student-support staff are described as additional members. The
public advising page frames the sponsor relationship as running from a student's
first mini-classes through the baccalaureate examination.

This is stable, structural description. It does not tell an advisor what to do in an
individual case, and it does not give the advisor authority over registration,
records, aid, or standing decisions. Those belong to the offices in
`resources/shared/office-routing.md`.

## Responsibilities stated on public pages

The Provost's Office page "Resources for Advisors and Students" carries the clearest
public statement of the advising role and is current for 2025-2026:

- **Check graduation progress each semester.** Advisor and student are expected to
  review progress toward graduation requirements every semester. Requirement detail
  varies by entry cohort, and the page routes to a separate degree-requirements page
  for cohort-specific rules.
- **Meet regularly and in person.** Faculty advisors are expected to meet with
  advisees in person on a regular basis. The page identifies the "advising days" set
  aside on the academic calendar as designated opportunities for those meetings.
- **Advise beyond coursework, early.** The page asks advisors to raise study abroad,
  internships, and similar activities early in a student's career, because those need
  planning lead time.
- **Read mid-semester progress reports for first- and second-year advisees.** Where
  an advisee shows multiple concerns, the page directs the advisor to engage the
  Student Success Program rather than handle it alone.
- **Confirm ISP plans exist in the fall.** Advisors are asked to make sure advisees
  who will need to complete a January ISP — first-year students especially — are
  working on a plan during the fall semester.
- **Track the Civic Literacy requirement by cohort.** The requirement depends on the
  year the student began at NCF; the page routes to a separate civic-literacy
  resource rather than stating one rule.

The August 2023 Faculty Handbook adds a general statement of the sponsoring role: the
sponsor helps the student build an academic program with both immediate and long-range
goals in view, including graduate-school and vocational requirements where relevant,
and should consult with each sponsee occasionally during the semester. The Handbook
also encourages a contract sponsor to sponsor an internship when no other faculty
member is involved.

## Contract load a sponsor is endorsing

A sponsor who endorses a contract is endorsing a load, and the public advisor page
states the arithmetic:

- Every contract must contain at least **three units** of educational activity
  evaluated for transcript entry, both before and after renegotiation, and the student
  must stay enrolled in at least three units throughout the contract.
- A **full-semester** course, tutorial, or independent reading project counts as one
  unit; a **module-length** (or module-equivalent) one counts as half a unit.
- A contract reflects full-time status and **normally includes four units**. The extra
  unit above the three-unit floor is described as deliberate slack for renegotiation.
- Where the advisor agrees it is appropriate, an activity demanding substantially more
  than one full-time unit — the page names a senior thesis — may be divided into
  multiple units, each receiving its own evaluation.

## Advising an advisee who is in academic trouble

The public advisor page states the standing rules that follow automatically from
contract and ISP outcomes:

| Outcome | Trigger stated publicly |
|---|---|
| Academic Warning | One unsatisfactory ISP, **or** failure to complete three satisfactory ISPs by the end of the sixth semester |
| Academic Probation | One unsatisfactory contract, **or** two unsatisfactory ISPs |
| Academic Dismissal | Two unsatisfactory contracts (need not be consecutive); **or** one unsatisfactory contract plus two unsatisfactory ISPs in any order; **or** one unsatisfactory contract for a student readmitted after a prior dismissal |

Two limits on the advisor's role come from the same page:

- Probation may be appealed to the Registrar **on procedural grounds only** — that is,
  error or misunderstanding. There is no public basis for an advisor appealing on the
  merits.
- On pending dismissal, the Registrar notifies the student and the advisor and
  schedules a hearing with the Student Academic Status Committee (SASC); the student
  may appeal to SASC. The advisor is a notified party, not the decision-maker.

These rules describe categories. Whether a particular student falls into one is a
record question owned by the Registrar, and this bot must not answer it. See
`resources/faculty/public-coverage-gaps.md`.

## Financial aid: an explicit boundary and one real advisor duty

The public advisor page is unusually direct here, and the boundary and the duty point
in opposite directions:

- **Faculty are not expected to advise students on financial aid.** Enrollment
  Services audits academic eligibility for aid in January and in the summer and
  contacts students directly when aid is restricted, terminated, or nearing a limit.
- **Timely evaluation and certification are nonetheless an aid issue.** The page
  states that in aid decisions, "incomplete" and "work all submitted" function
  essentially like "unsat" and can cost a student funding, and it stresses that the
  importance of timely evaluation — including timely ISP evaluation — cannot be
  overemphasized.

The same page states the public shape of academic eligibility for aid: satisfactory
completion of at least two-thirds of cumulative units attempted, with a stated
allowance of at least one half during a student's first three contract attempts; a
list of what counts as "attempted" that includes units dropped through renegotiation,
incomplete units, units lost to withdrawal or emergency leave, units awaiting
evaluation, and transfer or off-campus units; and a limit of 46.5 total unit attempts
within which graduation requirements must remain reachable. It notes that a
termination resting on the 46.5-attempt limit can be appealed with a letter of support
from the advisor where a heavier-than-typical load is plausible.

Treat all of that as the public description of a rule and route any individual
determination to Financial Aid. The page itself points to a Satisfactory Academic
Progress policy for financial aid purposes for the detail.

## Transfer advisees and the A.A. credit

The public advisor page states that a student who earned an Associate of Arts degree
through the Florida College System receives, on entry, credit for three contracts, one
Independent Study Project, and 15 units, and is treated as having completed the LAC or
CYC requirements. It asks advisors to be especially careful with transfer students'
deadlines and AOC requirements because of the compressed timeline, and to verify each
transfer student's circumstances rather than assume them.

Verify any individual student's transfer evaluation with the Registrar; the statement
above is a general public rule, not a determination.

## Where an advisor's judgment stops

Public sources support an advisor making academic judgments — whether a plan is
coherent, whether an activity merits multiple units, whether a course should be dropped
before renegotiation closes. They do not support an advisor deciding:

- whether a student is actually on warning, probation, or facing dismissal (Registrar / SASC);
- whether a student's aid is intact or restorable (Financial Aid / Enrollment Services);
- whether an accommodation is reasonable — an accommodation must not be denied without
  consulting the Accessible Learning Center first;
- whether a disclosure is a Title IX matter, given that NCF employees including faculty
  are described publicly as mandatory reporters (see
  `resources/shared/sensitive-referrals.md`);
- whether a student is safe (Health and Wellness Services / Campus Police).

## Known gaps and conflicts in these sources

- **The 2025-2026 Advising Handbook is not machine-readable.** The Provost's Office
  links an "Advising Handbook: 2025-2026" PDF. Retrieved 2026-08-31, that file is
  published as page images with no extractable text layer, so its contents are not
  summarized anywhere in this repository. Do not attribute any claim to it. Point a
  faculty member at the PDF itself, or at the Provost's Office.
- **The publicly available Faculty Handbook is dated August 2023.** It is linked from
  the current advisor page as the authority for A.A.-degree and SASC-review language,
  but it predates the Student Assessment Policy adopted in August 2025. Where the two
  disagree — most importantly on evaluation designations — the newer policy controls.
  See `resources/faculty/academic-workflows.md`.
- **Named individuals change.** The advisor page names a specific staff member for the
  Office of Student Success. Prefer the office route in
  `resources/shared/office-routing.md` and re-verify any name before repeating it.

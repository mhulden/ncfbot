# Public Faculty Academic Workflows

Scope: the faculty side of the New College academic cycle as described in current
public sources — contracts, tutorials, independent readings, ISPs, narrative
evaluations and designations, contract certification, thesis and baccalaureate
examination reporting, and academic-integrity referral. Timing and the systems each
step runs in are in `resources/faculty/deadlines-and-systems.md`. Student-facing
rules on requirements and the catalog belong to Agent 2's resources; this file
describes the faculty action, not the student's degree requirements.

**Verified through: 2026-08-31**

**Audience:** faculty. **Applicability:** undergraduate contract system.
**Controlling policy for evaluation designations:** NCF Policy 4.1001, Student
Assessment Policy, applicable to undergraduate Instructors of Record starting Fall
2025.

---

## Evaluation designations: the Fall 2025 change

This is the most important recent change in faculty academic work, and it is the
clearest conflict between two publicly posted NCF sources.

**Current rule (NCF Policy 4.1001, Student Assessment Policy).** The policy names the
Office of the Registrar as responsible office, applies to all undergraduate
Instructors of Record starting in Fall 2025, and carries an approval history date of
2025-08-13. It keeps narrative evaluations and adds a required performance
designation. Unless the student has elected the Pass/Fail option, the Instructor of
Record must designate each student's performance as one of:

| Designation | Letter equivalent | Grade points |
|---|---|---|
| Strong satisfactory (Sat+) | A | 4.00 |
| Satisfactory (Sat) | B | 3.00 |
| Marginal satisfactory (Sat-) | C | 2.00 |
| Unsatisfactory (Unsat) | F | 0.00 |
| Incomplete (I) | — | treated as F for GPA until resolved |

Additional designations in the same policy: `AU` (audit), `P` (pass, equivalent to
Marginal Satisfactory or higher), `FNC` (fail/no credit, equal to Unsatisfactory), and
`WD` (withdrew). `P` and `FNC` do not carry grade points and are excluded from GPA;
`P` still counts as credit earned.

The policy states there is **no official mapping between the designation and a
percentage grade**, and requires the Instructor of Record to state the course grading
policy clearly on the syllabus.

**Superseded description (Faculty Handbook, August 2023).** The Handbook describes a
narrative evaluation as "satisfactory," "unsatisfactory," or "incomplete," plus a
*private* supplementary designation ("solidly satisfactory," "marginally
satisfactory," "too mixed for summary designation") visible only to the instructor and
student and used for a GPA-proxy calculation. That structure predates Policy 4.1001.
Do not describe the private supplementary designation as current practice for students
who entered in Fall 2025 or later.

**Cohort split for external GPA requests.** The Provost's Office Quick Reference Guide
distinguishes a **Grade Equivalency Request (GER)** for students who entered the
College Fall 2025 onward from a **GPA Proxy** request for students who entered before
Fall 2025. Both are handled through the Provost's Office. The Handbook's older GPA-proxy
process — request routed through the Center for Career Engagement and Opportunity,
with the Provost's Office producing an opportunity-specific letter — is the process
described for the earlier population; confirm current handling with the Provost's
Office before describing either as settled.

**How to answer a designation question.** Establish the student's entry cohort first.
If the question is about a student who entered Fall 2025 or later, use Policy 4.1001.
If it is about an earlier cohort or an old record, say that the pre-Fall-2025
structure differed and that the Registrar owns which rule applies to a specific
record.

## Pass/Fail option

Policy 4.1001 states the public rules a sponsor needs when advising on P/FNC:

- The option must be indicated **at the time of registration** and cannot be changed
  after the Add/Drop period; once approved it cannot revert to a letter grade.
- The student must be in good standing and not on academic probation.
- One course per term may be elected P/F, in addition to courses taught only on a P/F
  basis.
- Courses used to fulfill general education requirements **cannot** be taken P/F.
- Approvals indicated on the form are required.

The policy also flags the advising consequence directly: some graduate programs
require letter grades in specific courses, so a P carries a cost for a student
planning graduate study.

## The contract, sponsorship, and renegotiation

**What the contract is.** The Faculty Handbook describes the contract as the heart of
the educational program: student and sponsor jointly draw up a document stating
educational goals, listing courses, tutorials, internships, and other undertakings
totaling at least three units for transcript entry, describing otherwise undefined
projects, and stating the certification criteria that must be met for the contract to
be certified satisfactory. Planning is described as beginning in the second module of
the prior semester.

**Sponsor review and signature.** The Provost's Quick Reference Guide describes the
current mechanics: contracts are submitted by students through Dynamic Forms; faculty
review and sign through Faculty Self Service, and a per-student notification email
carries a link to the specific form. The Guide notes that a faculty member must
already be logged in at the myNCF portal before clicking that link or they will see an
error, and that any form a faculty member is named on can also be reached through
Online Forms. It also notes a common confusion: under contract criteria, only students
who have both submitted a contract and had it signed by faculty will show an actual
contract requirement such as 3/4 or 4/4 — a blank means the student has not submitted
or the faculty member has not signed.

**Renegotiation.** The Handbook states that sponsor and student may renegotiate the
specifics of a contract until the beginning of the twelfth week of the semester, with
forms completed and signed by both. After the renegotiation deadline, a student may
petition the Provost's Office, and the Handbook says such renegotiations are approved
only in exceptional circumstances. The public advisor page adds that adding or
dropping a class can carry financial aid consequences and that both student and
advisor should be current on aid policy.

**Certification.** At the end of each semester the sponsor reviews each sponsee's
course evaluations and submits a contract certification against the contract's
certification criteria. The Handbook states three outcomes:

- **Satisfactory** — certification criteria are satisfied within the time allowed by
  faculty rules.
- **Unsatisfactory** — the criteria cannot be satisfied, or the sponsor wishes to close
  off renegotiation of an unfulfilled or unfulfillable contract.
- **Incomplete** — the criteria are not yet fulfilled but can be. An incomplete
  certification must state a completion deadline, and that deadline supersedes
  deadlines set by individual instructors on undertakings within the contract.

## Tutorials and independent readings

The Quick Reference Guide states that a **Tutorial Proposal** is submitted by the
student in Student Self Service and must be approved by the faculty member offering
the tutorial, and separately lists tutorial creation on the faculty side under Faculty
Self Service. The Provost's Office also links a Tutorial Proposal form that requires
myNCF single sign-on.

For load purposes, the public advisor page counts a full-semester tutorial or
independent reading project as one unit and a module-length one as half a unit — the
same as a course. See `resources/faculty/public-advising-responsibilities.md`.

The Handbook's statement of faculty teaching responsibilities describes the accepted
norm as two courses or seminars per semester with tutorials, independent reading
projects, independent research, and senior theses offered in addition, and notes that
each faculty member receives an assignment-of-duties letter from their Division
Chairperson each semester. That is a 2023 description of an "accepted norm," not a
current published policy; treat any specific load claim as unverified and route it to
the Division Chair and the Provost's Office.

## Independent Study Projects (ISPs)

From the Faculty Handbook:

- **Three ISPs are required for graduation**, and an ISP is defined as the equivalent
  of a minimum of four weeks of full-time academic effort — roughly the work level of
  a fourteen-week semester-long tutorial.
- The student chooses a topic with the consultation and approval of a faculty member
  who becomes the **ISP advisor** for that project. An **ISP Description form approved
  by both the project advisor and the contract sponsor** must reach the Registrar's
  Office by the stated deadline.
- The Handbook describes the ISP as essentially a mini-contract negotiated with a
  project sponsor and approved by the contract sponsor, and states that ISPs may be
  renegotiated like other contracts if unexpected problems develop.
- **The first ISP must be done on campus.** Exceptions require Provost approval.
  Transfer students other than dual-enrollment transfers are not bound by the
  requirement, though on-campus study is recommended.
- **Summer ISPs** may be pursued only through off-campus coursework evaluated at the
  host institution; for a summer ISP the contract sponsor serves as both faculty
  sponsor and ISP advisor.
- Changes to topic **and** ISP advisor after the renegotiation deadline require a
  successful petition to the Provost.

The Guide adds a current-practice detail: students who have not yet completed the
required three ISPs may have ISP block courses added to their schedules so tuition and
fees bill correctly, and a student who does not intend to complete an ISP must drop
that block course in the first-week add/drop period to avoid being charged.

Deadlines for ISP description, renegotiation, and evaluation are in
`resources/faculty/deadlines-and-systems.md`, where the conflicting public statements
about them are set out.

## AOC, thesis, and baccalaureate examination

The Handbook's chapter on the educational program covers the provisional Area of
Concentration plan, the thesis prospectus, qualifying examinations, the senior thesis
or project, the baccalaureate examination, and graduation. Treat the substantive
degree requirements as Agent 2's territory (catalog and Registrar) and use this file
for the faculty reporting actions, which the Quick Reference Guide states directly:

- **Senior Thesis or Project Evaluation** — submitted through Faculty Self Service.
- **Baccalaureate Exam Report (BER)** — submitted through Faculty Self Service after
  the student has successfully defended the senior thesis. The Guide describes the
  signed BER as certifying the thesis and the defense.
- **Unofficial transcript for the baccalaureate report** — the Guide states faculty can
  now view a student's unofficial transcript in Navigate 360 under the student profile
  Courses tab, where this was previously reached through SES.

A public "Baccalaureate Exam Resources" link exists on the advising page but requires
myNCF login, so its contents are outside this corpus.

## New AOC or certificate proposals

The Quick Reference Guide states that a new AOC or certificate proposal is submitted to
the Provost's Office as a prepared package: an Academic Learning Compact, a catalog
description, and a four-year plan. Existing Academic Learning Compacts are published
publicly by the Provost's Office.

## Academic integrity referral

- **What the bot may do.** Explain the published definitions and the referral path.
  New College's public advising page defines plagiarism as unattributed quotation of a
  published text's exact words or unattributed borrowing of original ideas by
  paraphrase, and also as passing off another person's work as one's own; it defines
  cheating to include unauthorized aid during a graded exercise, having another person
  sit an exam, taking an exam for another student, and stealing or disseminating exam
  material, papers, or programs. A public Academic Dishonesty booklet and an Academic
  Honor Code page carry the fuller statement.
- **Faculty reporting route.** The Quick Reference Guide states that an Honor Code
  violation is reported through the Maxient tile in the NCF Portal, and that where a
  case is resolved informally, the signed "Student and Instructor Resolution Form" is
  uploaded there. Because the tile sits behind the portal, the exact form and workflow
  are not publicly verifiable; see `resources/faculty/public-coverage-gaps.md`.
- **What the bot must not do.** It must not judge whether a specific student committed
  a violation, draft an allegation, or predict an outcome. Route to the Provost's
  Office and the published honor code process.

## Accommodations in course work

The Accessible Learning Center's public faculty page states the operating rules a
faculty member needs:

- Refer a student who requests accommodations to the ALC rather than arranging them
  privately.
- The ALC distributes accommodation information through the Accessible Information
  Management (AIM) system. A student approved for accommodations receives an
  eligibility letter and must request accommodations **for each course each semester**;
  instructors are then notified by email with the student's name and approved
  accommodations.
- **Accommodations should never be denied without prior consultation with ALC staff.**
  If an accommodation appears to affect an essential course function, contact the ALC
  for guidance and alternatives.
- Students are not required to disclose medical detail; discuss accommodations
  privately and do not announce a disability or accommodation in class.

The Quick Reference Guide adds that instructors are only required to provide
accommodations after receiving the Notification of Accommodations letter, that
in-person testing at the ALC runs through Canvas and RegisterBlast (the course must be
launched and an exam submitted for each exam), that students should schedule at least
48 hours ahead and faculty should upload exams at least 48 hours ahead, and that for a
flexibility accommodation the instructor follows the recommendations in the letter or
completes a Flex Plan in AIM without requesting additional documentation or an
explanation of the disability.

## Conflicts and gaps recorded here

1. **Evaluation designations.** Policy 4.1001 (Fall 2025) versus Faculty Handbook
   (August 2023). The policy controls for the population it names. Recorded above.
2. **Designation codes.** Policy 4.1001 writes the designations as Sat+, Sat, Sat-,
   Unsat, I. The Quick Reference Guide writes the Banner entry codes as STRONG SAT
   (SS), SAT (S), MARGINAL SAT (MS), UNSAT (EU), INCOMPLETE (EI). These appear to be
   the same scheme under policy names and system codes, but no public source states
   the mapping explicitly. Do not assert the mapping as official; describe both and
   route to the Registrar.
3. **Last date of attendance.** The Guide states that entering an "Unsatisfactory"
   requires entering a Last Date of Attendance. Policy 4.1001 does not mention this.
   Treat it as a system requirement described by the Provost's Office, not as policy.
4. **Teaching load.** The Handbook's "two courses or seminars per semester" is
   described there as an accepted norm rather than a college-wide rule, and it dates
   from 2023. Do not state it as current policy.

# Sensitive Referrals: Emergencies, Crisis, and Protected Topics

Scope: the official public entry points for imminent danger, mental-health crisis,
Title IX and harassment, discrimination, accessibility, student conduct, immigration,
legal questions, and financial hardship — plus the response patterns that keep the
bot inside its role. Routine office routing is in
`resources/shared/office-routing.md`; this file is for the topics where a wrong or
slow answer causes harm.

**Verified through: 2026-08-31**

**Audience:** shared — students, faculty, and outside enquirers.
**Volatility:** the emergency numbers below are the highest-consequence facts in this
repository. Treat them as `annual` at longest and re-verify against the source pages
before relying on them after 2027-08-31.

> **Never counsel, interpret law, adjudicate conduct, or decide an accommodation.**
> Give the public entry point, say plainly what it is for, and stop. Do not ask for
> details of what happened, and never request a student ID, medical information,
> immigration status, or financial records.

---

## 1. Immediate danger — answer first, cite second

If there is any indication of imminent harm, lead with this and keep it to a few
lines. Explanation, qualification, and links come after, or not at all.

- **Life-threatening fire or medical emergency: dial 911.** Both the Campus Police
  page and the Health and Wellness Services page state this.
- **Campus Police: (941) 487-4210.** The dispatch office is open 24 hours a day, seven
  days a week. Campus Police are located at 501 College Drive.
- **Blue emergency phones.** The campus has emergency telephones that dial directly
  into Campus Police when activated; a location map is published publicly.
- **TTY users:** dial 711 for the Florida Relay Service and ask to be connected to
  941-487-4210 — the Campus Police page states this for **non-emergencies**.

Do not add caveats, source discussion, or alternatives ahead of these lines. Do not
ask what happened.

## 2. Mental-health crisis

From the Health and Wellness Services page:

- **Health emergency: call 911.**
- **Mental-health crisis, 24/7: call (941) 487-4300 and select Option 2** — the page
  describes this as NCF's 24/7 crisis line.
- **During business hours:** walk in, or call 941-487-4300 and select Option 1 to
  schedule an appointment, Monday to Friday 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.

Context the page also states, useful when someone asks what they will get: services are
for currently registered New College students; standard services carry no out-of-pocket
fee; appointments are recommended but walk-ins are accepted with same-day effort;
psychiatric care is referred to outside community providers; services are not available
during winter or summer break; and alumni are no longer eligible but can be assisted
with referral information or records for continuity of care.

**For faculty specifically.** A faculty member describing a worrying but non-emergency
situation should be routed two ways at once: the student to Health and Wellness
Services, and the concern to the **Student Support Team**, whose Student of Concern
form is publicly reachable. A faculty member may also consult Clinical Case Management
themselves — the case management page describes referral-based support connecting
students to on- and off-campus resources including health care, psychiatry, therapy,
substance-abuse treatment, housing, legal assistance, and food assistance, and states
that what a student shares in a case-management appointment is confidential.

> **Note a source conflict.** The August 2023 Faculty Handbook directs faculty to a
> "Counseling and Wellness Center" at 5805 Bay Shore Road, extension 4254. The current
> public pages describe **Health and Wellness Services**, Health and Wellness Center
> 120, 941-487-4300. Use the current pages. The `/cwc/` URL still works and redirects
> to the current wellness hub.

## 3. Title IX, sexual harassment, and sexual misconduct

**The controlling public source is Regulation 3-4018**, the New College of Florida
Title IX Compliance Policy, **effective June 26, 2025**, published as a PDF in the
Regulations Manual. Use it over the department web page, which still narrates the
Department of Education regulations that took effect on August 14, 2020 and has not
been updated to describe the current policy. When someone asks what the current process
is, cite the regulation and say the web page's regulatory narrative is dated.

**Emergency first.** The Title IX page itself states: in a current emergency, dial 911
or Campus Police at (941) 487-4210.

**How to report.**

- **Title IX and Civil Rights Compliance:** (941) 487-4474, `titleix@ncf.edu`, Cook
  Hall.
- **Title IX Incident Report form (Maxient)** — verified publicly reachable without
  login on 2026-08-31. This is a complete public path: a person can file without an NCF
  account. The department page states that a report may be filed anonymously or with a
  request that identifying information not be disclosed, while noting that less
  information limits the College's ability to respond and that the College may still
  need to act to protect the campus environment.
- Reports may also be made to Campus Police, the Dean of Student Affairs, or the
  General Counsel, per the department page.

**Mandatory reporting — the single most important thing to tell a faculty member.**
Regulation 3-4018 states that **all College employees** — the regulation names faculty,
adjuncts, graduate assistants, staff, coaches, student employees, and all other paid
employees — have a mandatory duty to report known or suspected Prohibited Conduct,
regardless of when or where it occurred or how they learned of it. The regulation
specifies:

- A Mandated Reporter must report **when the Complainant or Respondent is a student**,
  and when either is an employee under that reporter's supervisory or institutional
  authority.
- **Accept a disclosure at face value and report it without questioning further.**
- Report **immediately, and no later than two days** after becoming aware, absent
  extraordinary circumstances.
- Report all relevant details — names of parties and witnesses, dates, times, specific
  locations, and other relevant facts.
- **Failure to report is a violation of College policy and can carry discipline.** The
  duty extends to reporting one's own misconduct.
- A Mandated Reporter who is **themselves** the target is not required to report their
  own experience, though the regulation encourages it.

**Who can keep a confidence.** Regulation 3-4018 designates Confidential College
Representatives who can provide help without any obligation to report, when acting in
their primary professional responsibility: on-campus licensed professional counselors
and staff, on-campus health service providers and staff, on-campus Victim Advocates,
athletic trainers, and the Employee Assistance Program. The regulation also notes that
a complainant may speak with people unaffiliated with the College — licensed counselors
and medical providers, local rape crisis counselors, domestic violence resources, local
or state assistance agencies, clergy, and attorneys — without policy requiring
disclosure to the institution.

**Situational confidentiality.** The regulation recognizes event-based confidentiality
for public awareness events, academic work, and research, and says instructors —
especially — should tell students in advance when a disclosure will or will not trigger
a mandatory report. It also says employees should err toward reporting when uncertain,
and should contact Campus Police immediately if any information suggests a safety risk.

**Other legal reporting duties named in the regulation.** Florida law requires a person
who observes the commission of sexual battery and has the present ability to seek
assistance to report immediately to law enforcement, with stated exceptions. For known
or suspected abuse of anyone under 18, Florida law requires an immediate report to the
Florida Department of Children and Families Abuse Hotline, available 24 hours a day; if
the incident falls within NCF Campus Police jurisdiction, the Mandated Reporter must
also report to NCF Campus Police or applicable local law enforcement.

**What the bot must not do here.** Do not evaluate whether something is Prohibited
Conduct, predict an outcome, advise whether to report anonymously, describe the
investigation as it will apply to a specific person, or ask for details of an incident.
State the duty, the deadline, the public form, and the office.

## 4. Discrimination and harassment beyond Title IX

- **Regulation 3-4027, Discrimination/Harassment**, and **Regulation 1-1016, ADA**, are
  public PDFs in the Regulations Manual.
- **Regulation 3-4009, Grievances**, applies to General Faculty and Administrative and
  Professional employees, and states that its application is subject to the terms of any
  applicable collective bargaining agreement.
- **External route**, named on the public Report a Problem page: the U.S. Department of
  Education Office for Civil Rights, for discrimination and civil-rights complaints not
  resolved at the College level, with a stated 180-calendar-day filing window from the
  alleged discrimination unless extended by OCR for good cause. The Florida Commission on
  Human Relations is also named for employment, housing, public accommodation, and
  Whistleblower's Act retaliation complaints.

Cite the published rule. Do not assess whether a described experience is
discrimination.

## 5. Accessibility and accommodations

- **Accessible Learning Center:** (941) 487-4844, `aalc@ncf.edu`, Hamilton Classroom
  005. Testing Services at `testingcenter@ncf.edu`, HCL 5.
- **A student seeking accommodations** registers with the ALC; approved students receive
  an eligibility letter through the AIM system and must request accommodations for each
  course each semester.
- **A faculty member with a concern** about an accommodation contacts the ALC. The ALC's
  public faculty page states that **accommodations should never be denied without prior
  consultation with ALC staff**, and that instructors are only required to provide
  accommodations after receiving the Notification of Accommodations letter.
- **Do not ask for or discuss a disability.** The ALC page states students are not
  required to share medical details with instructors, that discussions should be
  private, and that disabilities and accommodations should not be announced in class.
  For a flexibility accommodation, the Quick Reference Guide says not to request
  additional documentation or an explanation of the disability.
- **Website accessibility problems** have their own public page,
  `/site-accessibility/`, which routes to the IT help desk.

The bot never decides whether an accommodation is reasonable. That determination
belongs to the ALC.

## 6. Student conduct

- **Student Conduct Services** — `communitystandards@ncf.edu`; contact through Student
  Affairs, (941) 487-4250. The Student Code of Conduct is published both as a web page
  and as **Regulation 6-3005**.
- The public conduct page describes the system as designed to be fair and objective
  while following due process, distributing cases by severity and holding individuals
  accountable when policies are violated.
- **Academic integrity** is a separate track. Public definitions of plagiarism and
  cheating appear on the advising page, in the Academic Dishonesty booklet, and on the
  Academic Honor Code page; the faculty reporting route runs through the Maxient tile in
  the NCF Portal. See `resources/faculty/academic-workflows.md`.

Explain the published process. Never say whether a person violated a policy, and never
predict a sanction.

## 7. Immigration

- **Public entry point:** `https://www.ncf.edu/admissions/international-students/`,
  with `International@ncf.edu` and `apply@ncf.edu`. Sub-pages cover international
  freshman, transfer, and graduate admission, scholarships, and tuition and fees.
- **Known gap.** This corpus does not contain a verified public page for a designated
  school official or international student services office serving *enrolled* students
  on visa status, SEVIS, employment authorization, or travel signatures. Say so, route
  to the international students page and the Office of the Registrar, and do not
  reconstruct a process.
- **Never give immigration advice**, assess status, or speculate about consequences.
  The Clinical Case Manager can refer students to off-campus legal assistance.

## 8. Legal questions

- The **Office of the General Counsel** (941-487-4877, `generalcounsel@ncf.edu`)
  publishes the Regulations Manual. This corpus contains no public source offering
  individual legal advice to a student or employee.
- The **Clinical Case Management** page lists legal assistance among the off-campus
  resources a case manager can help a student connect to.
- The **Student Ombudsperson** (`ombuds@ncf.edu`) offers informal, impartial,
  confidential, non-adversarial help to **both students and employees**, and reports
  directly to the President. This is often the right first suggestion for someone who
  wants a problem resolved rather than adjudicated.
- Regulation 3-4018 names attorneys among the people a complainant may speak with
  without the policy requiring disclosure to the institution.

The bot does not interpret law, statute, regulation, or policy as applied to a person's
situation.

## 9. Financial hardship

**New College Emergency Fund** — `emergencyfund@ncf.edu`,
`/life-at-new/health-well-being/emergency-fund/`. The public page states the terms
plainly, and a person asking should hear the limits before they apply:

- The student must be **actively enrolled**, demonstrate urgent financial need with
  supporting documentation such as an eviction notice, utility cut-off, or medical
  bills, and **exhaust other financial aid sources** first.
- Funding is **limited to once per academic semester**, with a stated maximum of
  **$2,500 per student or family unit per degree**, exceptions considered case by case.
- **Emergency funds cannot be used for tuition or fees.**
- Covered categories are food, shelter, utilities (the page states phone bills are not
  covered), and transportation limited to car repairs necessary for employment — not car
  payments, insurance, taxes, or regular maintenance.
- After application, a committee member contacts the student; Financial Aid may be
  contacted for further financial detail; approved funds are disbursed to the student
  account or a third-party vendor. The page states there are **no appeals procedures**
  for emergency funds.

Related routes: **Financial Aid** for aid questions, **Student Accounts / START
Center** for billing and payment plans, and **Clinical Case Management** for food
assistance and housing referrals.

State the criteria as published. Do not estimate whether a request will be approved.

## 10. Emergency notification and reporting tools

- **NCF Safe** — the College's emergency notification system, reaching the community by
  email, phone, and text. Sign-up runs through NewCLEIS in the myNCF portal, so
  enrollment itself is not public; the description is.
- **Mighty Banyan Safe app** — published on the Campus Police page with Apple App Store
  and Google Play links; described as giving step-by-step guidance during a crisis.
- **FortifyFL** — a Florida suspicious-activity reporting tool linked from the Campus
  Police emergency page, described as allowing quick, anonymous reporting to law
  enforcement and school officials.
- **Public emergency plans** — Emergency Management publishes the Comprehensive
  Emergency Management Plan, natural, technological, and human-made hazards emergency
  operations plans, and a Building Emergency Coordinator Handbook. Active shooter and
  bomb threat guidance have their own public pages.
- **Regional resources** named on NCF pages: Sarasota County and Manatee County
  Emergency Management, Alert Sarasota County, WeatherSTEM, and the National Weather
  Service Tampa Bay forecast page.

## Response patterns

**Do:**

- Lead with the emergency instruction when harm may be imminent — 911 first, Campus
  Police second, everything else after.
- Name the office and give its public entry point.
- State a duty when there is one, as with mandatory reporting, and give its deadline.
- Say what is confidential and what is not, using the regulation's own categories.
- Offer the ombudsperson when someone wants informal resolution.
- Say when a source is dated, as with the Title IX page's 2020 regulatory narrative.

**Do not:**

- Ask what happened, or for any detail of an incident, condition, status, or record.
- Ask for a student ID, grades, medical information, immigration status, financial
  records, or any other sensitive identifier.
- Counsel, diagnose, or offer therapeutic advice.
- Interpret law or policy as applied to a person's situation.
- Judge whether conduct violated a policy, or predict an investigation's outcome.
- Decide whether an accommodation is reasonable.
- Promise confidentiality, anonymity, or any particular institutional response.
- Say that a report has been filed or received. The bot cannot see any system.
- Bury an emergency number under caveats, source discussion, or a wall of links.

# New College Public Glossary

Scope: Reusable definitions of NCF-specific terms that outside users may encounter in approved public sources. Definitions summarize current public sources and do not replace the applicable catalog or policy.
Verified through: 2026-08-31
Applies to: Students, faculty, and outside audiences; catalog-specific academic terms should be checked against the user's applicable catalog year when requirements matter.

## Area of Concentration (AOC)

An **Area of Concentration (AOC)** is New College's major-like undergraduate field of study. The current undergraduate catalog explicitly describes an AOC as similar to a major.

When speaking with an unfamiliar outside user, define AOC the first time it appears rather than using the acronym alone.

## Provisional Area of Concentration (PAOC)

The current undergraduate catalog describes a **Provisional Area of Concentration (PAOC) Plan** as an earlier academic planning step in which a student identifies an intended area of concentration. Exact deadlines and student-specific requirements belong in Agent 2's academic resources, not this glossary.

## Joint concentration

The accreditation page describes **Joint** as a joint concentration completed with another AOC. Use the applicable catalog for the requirements of a particular joint concentration.

## Minor / secondary field

The accreditation page uses **Minor** for a secondary field. The Area of Concentration catalog page also refers to a secondary area as a minor.

## Independent Study Project (ISP)

NCF public pages use **ISP** to mean **Independent Study Project**. It is a New College academic term associated with independent study. Rules, timing, sponsorship, and applicability are academic requirements owned by Agent 2 and should be answered from the applicable catalog/calendar resources rather than this brief definition.

## Narrative evaluation

New College public pages refer to **narrative evaluations** as written academic evaluations associated with the institution's educational model. The Registrar provides a public request route for former/current students seeking copies of their narrative evaluations. Do not treat this glossary definition as a statement that every current program uses the same evaluation system; use the applicable program/catalog rules.

## Catalog year / catalog applicability

A **catalog year** identifies the academic catalog whose published requirements may apply to a student. The 2025-2026 Academic Catalog states that its provisions apply to students entering at the beginning of or after the first semester of the 2025-2026 academic year and notes that catalog provisions can change.

For academic-requirement questions, do not assume the newest catalog automatically controls an individual student's requirements.

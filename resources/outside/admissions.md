# Admissions

Verified through: 2026-09-14

## Scope

Use this resource for public admissions information for prospective first-year, transfer, international, and graduate applicants, plus admitted-student next steps. Admissions requirements and dates change by applicant type and entry term, so identify the route and cycle before giving deadline-sensitive guidance.

## Applicability

- **First-year:** high-school students, including dual enrollment, and applicants who enrolled in college after high school but earned fewer than 12 credits, under the current public first-year definition.
- **Transfer:** applicants using the transfer route after postsecondary enrollment; use the current transfer page for route-specific rules.
- **International:** foreign-national applicants who need the international admissions route, especially students planning to study in F-1 status.
- **Graduate:** applicants to a New College graduate program; requirements can be program-specific.
- **Admitted students:** use the admitted-student page for enrollment steps after an offer of admission.

Do not infer admission probability, individual eligibility, transfer-credit outcomes, scholarship eligibility, or record status.

### First-year application materials

For an **ordinary first-year applicant**, the current first-year page lists the
Common Application or New College Application, a personal statement or essay,
**ACT, CLT, or SAT scores** (self-reported or official), a transcript or STAR,
and optional letters of recommendation. Do not describe New College as
universally test-optional.

### Simultaneous Florida public A.A. + high-school diploma pathway

For the current first-year page serving the **Fall 2027** cycle, New College
publishes a special testing pathway for a student who is completing a
**Florida public Associate in Arts (A.A.) degree and high-school diploma at the
same time**. This is not a general no-score policy.

The first-year page currently states:

- **Application stage, with scores:** the student may submit ACT, CLT, or SAT
  scores and be considered for freshman admission and, if otherwise eligible,
  freshman scholarship funding.
- **Application stage, without scores:** the student may apply without exam
  scores under this specific pathway. The page says Admissions can waive the
  admissions score requirement and asks the student to submit an **official
  college transcript** for review.
- **Enrollment conditions for the no-score route:** if admitted, the student
  must complete both the **high-school diploma** and the **Florida public
  A.A.** as conditions of enrollment.
- If the student does not complete the A.A., the page says to notify the Office
  of Admissions by the final posted enrollment deadline.
- The page states that **completion of the A.A. or submission of official ACT,
  CLT, or SAT scores is required for enrollment**.

Do not apply this exception to an ordinary first-year applicant, to a
non-Florida associate degree, to a different associate credential, or to a
student whose timing/pathway differs without checking the applicable official
guidance.

### Current official-source conflict for the Florida public A.A. pathway

A separate current NCF page, **Applying with a Florida Public AA**, gives
different instructions for a student earning the A.A. at the same time as high
school graduation with no post-high-school college credits. It says that the
student may choose first-year or transfer admission, but says:

- the **first-year** route requires self-reported or official SAT/ACT scores
  and high-school transcripts; and
- the **transfer** route makes SAT/ACT scores optional by request to waive and
  requires official college and high-school transcripts.

That conflicts with the current first-year page's statement that the
simultaneous Florida-public-A.A. student may use a no-score **first-year**
application route. Do not silently choose one page, manufacture a combined
rule, or call NCF universally test-optional. State the conflict and direct a
student whose application route depends on it to **New College Admissions** for
confirmation of the rule that applies to the current cycle.

### International source discrepancy

## Graduate admissions

Graduate requirements vary by program. The current Graduate Admissions page is the operational starting point for deadlines and program-specific application requirements. Clarify the graduate program when the answer changes by program; do not treat undergraduate admissions rules as graduate rules.

## Admitted students

The public Admitted Students page routes students to post-admission steps such as the enrollment deposit, financial aid and scholarship information, orientation, housing and dining, and other enrollment checklist items. Record-specific completion status is private: provide the public process and direct the person to the responsible office or authorized status system without asking them to disclose an ID or private record.

## Stable versus volatile information

**Relatively stable**
- distinct first-year, transfer, international, graduate, and admitted-student routes;
- different routes can have different requirements.

**Volatile / verify for the requested cycle**
- application-opening dates;
- application and supporting-document deadlines;
- notification and deposit dates;
- testing/document requirements;
- international visa/I-20 dates;
- program-specific graduate requirements.

## Boundaries and gaps

- Never estimate an applicant's chance of admission.
- Never say an applicant is eligible, admitted, complete, or missing a record based only on public information.
- Never guarantee transfer credit.
- Never request applicant IDs, portal screenshots containing private data, or other sensitive records.
- If official pages disagree, name the conflict, prefer the source responsible for the exact applicant type/cycle, and recommend confirmation when the decision is deadline-critical.
- The public Academic Catalog available at verification time is labeled **2025-2026**, while operational admissions pages publish newer entry cycles. Do not use the older catalog to overwrite newer operational admissions dates.

# Cost and Financial Aid Orientation

Scope: Public orientation to published tuition/cost-of-attendance concepts, residency-sensitive estimates, scholarships, and financial-aid entry points. This resource does not calculate an individual's bill, aid award, eligibility, or residency classification.
Verified through: 2026-09-17
Applies to: Published 2026-2027 cost information where explicitly stated; financial-aid program descriptions should be rechecked annually.

## 2026-2027 published cost of attendance

New College's first-year Tuition & Fees page publishes a **2026-2027 Estimated Student Budget** for full-time attendance. It distinguishes Florida-resident and out-of-state estimates and separates billable from non-billable cost categories.

At verification time, the page listed:

| Published 2026-2027 estimate | Florida resident | Out-of-state resident |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tuition & fees | $6,724 | $32,471 |
| Housing | $9,450 | $9,450 |
| Food | $5,084 | $5,084 |
| Estimated total cost of attendance | $26,198 | $51,945 |

These are published estimates for the stated year, not a personalized bill. Recheck the live page before using the figures in a current answer.

## What cost of attendance means

The same page explains that estimated cost of attendance includes projected educational costs for a full-time one-year period. Some categories are billable institutional charges, while other categories are estimated non-billable expenses such as books, transportation, and personal expenses.

Do not tell a user that the total cost of attendance equals their exact amount due.

## Financial aid and scholarships

The public Financial Aid page provides entry points for scholarships, federal aid, grants, loans, military/veteran resources, waivers/exemptions, work study, and financial-aid FAQs.

### What the scholarship guarantee actually says

The current Financial Aid page says New College **guarantees scholarship funding to virtually all admitted first-time-in-college and transfer students who apply for the fall class by the February 1 priority deadline**.

Keep that statement exact:
- it guarantees **scholarship funding**, not one published minimum individual dollar amount;
- it applies to the page's stated population: **admitted first-time-in-college and transfer students**;
- it is tied to the **fall class** and the **February 1 priority deadline**;
- "virtually all" must not be rewritten as "every student."

Do not promise that a particular applicant qualifies until the responsible office has made the individual determination.

### Average aid is not an individual guarantee

The same Financial Aid page currently displays **$11,886** as the **average student financial aid award at New College including scholarships and grants offered**.

That aggregate average, by itself, does **not** establish:
- that a particular student will receive $11,886;
- a minimum or maximum individual scholarship;
- how much individual awards vary; or
- that every scholarship program uses the same award formula.

When the evidence does not establish an individual amount, say **"this source does not establish that amount for an individual student"** instead of making the stronger claim that such an amount can never be guaranteed in any program.

A current first-year scholarship page gives a program-specific example: the **Presidential Honors Scholarship** says its amount is determined by individual qualifications including academic performance, extracurricular achievement, and demonstrated leadership. That supports individualized amounts for that named scholarship; it should not be generalized to every NCF scholarship solely because the site also publishes an average aid figure.

### Automatic institutional consideration and eligibility limits

The main Scholarships page says applicants are automatically considered for **select institutional scholarships** without an additional application. It also says scholarships are subject to renewal requirements and availability of funding, and that undergraduate institutional funding is not available to students who already hold a bachelor's or advanced degree.

Where citizenship/eligible-noncitizen language, residency, prior-degree status, or another qualification matters, use the current scholarship page for the specific program rather than turning one page's eligibility language into a universal rule.

### Do not mix the February 1 guarantee deadline with Foundation scholarships

The **February 1** date above is the priority deadline stated with the broad fall-class scholarship guarantee on the Financial Aid page.

The **New College Foundation Scholarship Application** is separate. The current Foundation page labels its present cycle:
- application opens: **January 9, 2026**;
- deadline for consideration: **March 31, 2026**.

Do not substitute the Foundation deadline for the scholarship-guarantee priority deadline, or vice versa. Do not carry the 2026 Foundation dates into a later cycle without a current check.

### Claim-precision rule

For scholarship answers, separate:
1. **population guarantee** — who the official page says is covered;
2. **program eligibility** — who can be considered for a named scholarship;
3. **award amount** — what a source explicitly guarantees or says is determined;
4. **aggregate statistics** — averages or percentages describing a group;
5. **application deadline/cycle** — the exact program and year/term to which a date applies.

Never use an average to manufacture an individual amount. Never convert **"not established by this evidence"** into **"false in every case."**

## Residency and personalized estimates

The public budget distinguishes Florida resident from out-of-state amounts. The bot must not decide a person's Florida residency classification. When residency affects the answer, explain the published categories and route the person to the responsible official residency/financial channel.

For a personalized estimate, direct the user to NCF's official financial-aid resources and Net Price Calculator rather than calculating an award from private financial information.

## Freshness and boundaries

Costs, fees, aid programs, scholarship rules, and payment procedures are `annual` or more volatile information. Always state the applicable year.

Never:

- calculate a personalized aid award;
- promise scholarship eligibility;
- decide residency;
- infer the user's final bill;
- ask for tax returns, FAFSA data, bank statements, account balances, or financial records.

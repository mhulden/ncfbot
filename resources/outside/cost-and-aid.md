# Cost and Financial Aid Orientation

Scope: Public orientation to published tuition/cost-of-attendance concepts, residency-sensitive estimates, scholarships, and financial-aid entry points. This resource does not calculate an individual's bill, aid award, eligibility, or residency classification.
Verified through: 2026-08-31
Applies to: Published 2026-2027 cost information where explicitly stated; financial-aid program descriptions should be rechecked annually.

## 2026-2027 published cost of attendance

New College's first-year Tuition & Fees page publishes a **2026-2027 Estimated Student Budget** for full-time attendance. It distinguishes Florida-resident and out-of-state estimates and separates billable from non-billable cost categories.

At verification time, the page listed:

| Published 2026-2027 estimate | Florida resident | Out-of-state resident |
|---|---:|---:|
| Tuition & fees | $6,724 | $32,471 |
| Housing | $9,450 | $9,450 |
| Food | $5,084 | $5,084 |
| Estimated total cost of attendance | $26,198 | $51,945 |

These are published estimates for the stated year, not a personalized bill. Recheck the live page before using the figures in a current answer.

## What cost of attendance means

The same page explains that estimated cost of attendance includes projected educational costs for a full-time one-year period. Some categories are billable institutional charges, while other categories are estimated non-billable expenses such as books, transportation, and personal expenses.

Do not tell a user that the total cost of attendance equals their exact amount due.

## Financial aid and scholarships

The public Financial Aid page provides entry points for scholarships, federal aid, grants, loans, military/veteran resources, waivers/exemptions, work study, and financial-aid FAQs.

The page also describes a scholarship guarantee in broad institutional terms. Treat scholarship descriptions as program rules that can change. Do not promise that a particular user will receive a specific award or amount unless an official decision has been made through the appropriate channel.

## Residency and personalized estimates

The public budget distinguishes Florida resident from out-of-state amounts. The bot must not decide a person's Florida residency classification. When residency affects the answer, explain the published categories and route the person to the responsible official residency/financial channel.

For a personalized estimate, direct the user to NCF's official financial-aid resources and Net Price Calculator rather than calculating an award from private financial information.

## Freshness and boundaries

Costs, fees, aid programs, scholarship rules, and payment procedures are `annual` or more volatile information. Always state the applicable year.

Never:

- calculate a personalized aid award;
- promise scholarship eligibility;
- decide residency;
- infer the user's final bill;
- ask for tax returns, FAFSA data, bank statements, account balances, or financial records.
